Ethical Concerns & Rules of Conduct

As we integrate tech, we must maintain high ethical standards:

  • Data Privacy: Protecting student data from unauthorized commercial use.
  • Academic Integrity: Ensuring AI tools are used for assistance, not for plagiarism.
  • Equity: Bridging the "Digital Divide" so that low-income students aren't left behind.
  • Algorithmic Bias: Ensuring educational software doesn't reinforce social stereotypes.

1. Accuracy and Honesty (Integrity)

Fact-Checking: You have a responsibility to ensure the information on your five pages is true. Presenting "fake news" or unverified rumors as facts is an ethical violation. Misleading Content: Avoid using "clickbait" titles that don't match the actual content of the page.

2. Intellectual Property and Plagiarism

Giving Credit: If you use text, research, or data from another source, you must cite it. Taking someone else’s work and presenting it as your own is unethical (and often a violation of school policy). Image Licensing: Using copyrighted images without permission is a common ethical (and legal) slip-up. Using royalty-free images or creative commons graphics is the ethical way to handle "graphics and sound elements."

3. Privacy and Data Security

User Data: If your website has a "Contact Us" form, an ethical concern is how you handle the names and emails of people who fill it out. You shouldn't sell their data or use it for anything other than what they intended. No Malicious Code: Ensuring your site is safe and doesn't contain links to viruses or phishing sites.

4. Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (Socially Acceptable)

Avoiding Bias: Your content should not promote hate speech, discrimination, or harmful stereotypes against any group of people based on race, religion, gender, or ability. Accessibility: Making sure your website is usable for people with disabilities (e.g., using "Alt Text" for images so visually impaired people can use screen readers) is considered an ethical design practice.

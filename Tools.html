    1. Learning Management Systems (LMS) The foundation of digital learning is the LMS. Platforms like Google Classroom, Canvas, and Moodle serve as virtual hubs where students access assignments, submit work, and communicate with instructors. These tools ensure that all learning materials are organized in one central, accessible location.

    2. Productivity & Organization Suites To handle the high volume of digital information, students rely on organization tools like Notion or Trello for project management. For document creation and real-time collaboration, suites like Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 are indispensable, allowing multiple students to edit the same report simultaneously from different locations.

    3. Research & AI Assistants Artificial Intelligence has introduced a new tier of tools. Grammarly helps refine writing, while AI-powered research assistants like Perplexity or Zotero help students organize citations and find academic sources faster. These tools act as "co-pilots," allowing students to focus more on critical thinking and less on repetitive formatting tasks.
